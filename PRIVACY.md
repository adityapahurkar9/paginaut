# Paginaut privacy policy

Paginaut is a reader and editor for PDF, e-books, comics, XPS and Markdown, for Android, Windows and Linux. It
doesn't collect, store on servers, or share any information about you. There are no accounts, ads or tracking.

## Android

The Android app has no internet access: it can't send anything anywhere.

**What stays on your phone:** the documents you open, Paginaut's copies of documents other apps send it, your
reading positions, bookmarks, the list of books on your shelf, your settings, and a log of the app's own errors. If
Android's backup is on, Android may save Paginaut's settings and reading positions to your Google account; the
developer never receives them.

**Files:** Paginaut only reads documents you open, pick, or keep in folders you choose for its shelf. If you turn on
"All files access", it looks for e-books and comics anywhere on the phone. It never changes, moves or deletes your
files.

**Read aloud** uses your phone's own text-to-speech voice. **Share** and **Print** send a document only where you
choose.

## Windows and Linux

The desktop app connects to the internet only for what you ask of it, and only to GitHub and the sources named
here:

- **Updates:** it asks github.com for the newest release (once a day if you leave that on in Settings › About, or
  when you choose Check for updates). Nothing about you or your files is sent.
- **Downloads you choose:** text-recognition languages (from GitHub), read-aloud voices (from Hugging Face) and
  LibreOffice (from The Document Foundation). Each file is checked against the signed list that ships with the app.
- **Links** in documents open in your browser when you click them.

Your documents, settings, signatures and everything else stay on your computer. Saved signatures are kept
encrypted with your system's key store.

## Problem reports (all platforms)

Paginaut keeps a log of its own errors on your device (what went wrong and when), with file paths and document
names replaced by placeholders such as `<file>.pdf`. It is never sent automatically.

If you choose Report a problem, Paginaut shows you exactly what a report contains and lets you choose whether to
include your system details (app version, system and version, processor type, memory, graphics, screen size,
language; on Android also the phone's maker and model) and the error log. You then send it yourself: as a public
GitHub issue that you post in your browser, or (Android) through an app you pick, or (desktop) by copying or saving
it. What you post on GitHub is public and is covered by GitHub's own privacy statement.
